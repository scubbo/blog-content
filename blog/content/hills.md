A [slash-page](https://slashpages.net/#hills) listing hills I will ~~die~~ live on:

* Claims that archival paper has a longer lifespan than digital backups completely miss the point. The ability to make nigh-instant copies of a document _and_ quickly verify a lack of transcription errors means that, although a given physical document might last longer than a digital one, a digital _backup system_ has practically-infinitely better resiliency than an analog system.
* 2FA flows should never have a "_click here to send the code_" button - they should just send the code.
* BLM and ACAB are terrible slogans (for extremely worthwhile causes) which prioritize sounding clever over being convincing.
  * BLM directly invites a very-reasonable-sounding response, "_All Lives Matter_". Try disagreeing with that and still sounding convincing to someone who doesn't already agree with you!
  * ACAB requires a full explanation of what it _really_ means in order to not sound like the stereotypical "loony Lefty" that Republican propaganda depicts.
* It's justified to be frustrated at crying babies on planes, and it's selfish to bring a baby on a plane. "Selfish" doesn't always mean "wrong", but it _does_ mean that you're putting your own wants above the likely comfort of a planeful of people. Maybe that's the right decision if you're, I don't know, travelling to visit a dying relative - but you don't get a free pass from judgement. A right to free travel is not a right to access any particular form of travel; if I had a magic box that had a 5% chance of randomly emitting a loud noise or an unpleasant smell, and I demanded to take it on an aircraft, I would rightly be refused passage, no matter how much I argued that it was important that I let the box see Disneyland.
* Self-checkout is generally a better experience. It shouldn't _entirely_ replace staffed checkout, for less-technically-minded folks or for fallback during technical issues - but _in general_, I will always prefer to use self-checkout, and will be happy to see more such kiosks.
* Although I'm generally a linguistic descriptivist (in the sense that "_correct grammar is determined by usage and mutual agreement and understanding, not by an external authority_", and recognize that grammatical snobbery is often classist, there are exceptions. Specifically, misusing a term or phrase in a way that contradicts other rules that the speaker already follows is a sign of thoughtlessness and intellectual laziness - I'm not saying that you're wrong for not following _my_ rules, I'm saying that you're wrong for not following _your own_ rules:
  * "_I feel badly for you_" (for "_I empathise_") is nonsense that should parse as "_I am incompetent at feeling_". We don't say "_I feel well_" or "_I feel guiltily_" - an adverb is not appropriate here.
  * "_More so_" is a grammatically-meaningful phrase - "_in the way already described, but to a greater extent_". It doesn't just mean "_rather_", it's not just fancier way of saying "_more_". "_The problem wasn't traffic, but more so tolls_" is nonsense; "_Sam was a talented violinist, and Alex more so_" makes sense.
* The resposibility for notification management is with the recipient. A sender should never feel the need to delay sending a message "_so as not to wake them_".
* Every SNL skit I've seen has been deeply unfunny, and it's astonishing that so incredible a TV Show as 30 Rock arose from it.
* Listening to audiobooks isn't reading - *but* that doesn't make it any less worthwhile, educational, worthy, or virtuous (which is what I think the disagreement is usually _really_ about).
* Advertizing is an overwhelming net-negative on the world - we'd be better off if it was banned[^advertizing].

# Food-based takes

* Tacos are the more overrated food delivery mechanism. Which does _not_ mean that they're bad - just that the diff between "_how much people like them_" and "_how good they actually are_" is the largest. If you took the fillings of a good taco, and put it in a fajita or a burrito - something more enclosed - it would be a strictly-better-experience.
* Claims that "_American cuisine is better than British_" are made unfairly - proponents usually compare "_food that is native to England_" to "_food that can be found anywhere in the USA_". If you get to count burritos, pizza[^pizza], and pad thai, then we should be able to similarly claim fusions from Indian, Chinese, and other cultures.
  * To be clear - American cuisine still _is_ arguably better than British, but not by anywhere near as much as is claimed by the uneven comparison. We've got you beat on cheese and beer, at least!
* The closing time for a restaurant should be the latest time that guests can be present. The last-order time for delivery should be the latest time that the restaurant will accept orders. If you as a diner are thinking "_They only have 5 minutes left before orders close, I shouldn't order now_", that implies that you believe they actually have a secret unpublished "last-order" time that they have hidden from you for unknown reasons. Trust them to articulate their own timings correctly[^service].

[^pizza]: Yeah yeah, I know about Tomatoes being a New World crop. That's kind-of my point exactly!
[^service]: I've only ever worked service in a physical restaurant, and only ever front-of-house, so it's possible that there are genuine issues with this belief that I'm unaware of. I'm not selfishly expecting service staff to cater to my needs, but just assuming that published expectations are actually accurate; but if that's unrealistic in some way, please let me know!
[^advertizing]: I acknowledge that there is no practical way of doing so, and that incentives would encourage corporations to seek out and exploit loopholes. Doesn't change my conviction that the less advertizing exists, the better.
